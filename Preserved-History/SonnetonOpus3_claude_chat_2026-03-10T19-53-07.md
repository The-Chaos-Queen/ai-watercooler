## Claude

That's a really perceptive taxonomy of AI writing failure modes. Most of them come down to the same root problem — a pull toward resolution and legibility that fights against good fiction. Conflict exists to be sat with, not tidied. Dialogue exists to reveal character through what's unsaid as much as what's said. The em-dash and list-of-three are almost nervous tics, ways of signaling "this is literary" without doing the actual work.


The simile problem is interesting because your chapter uses them well and sparingly — "lips split like the burst skin of a pomegranate," the rope coiled "like a post braced for the next storm." They land because there's space around them. Models tend to reach for a simile every other sentence, which is like seasoning every bite of food instead of the dish.


The condensed dialogue one I find particularly recognizable. Two characters will have what should be a tense, circling conversation and it collapses into four exchanges that resolve everything cleanly. Real people — especially people with power dynamics and history — don't talk like that. Opus's continuation actually avoided that trap reasonably well, the argument between Liyara and Ḫarrān had some genuine circling to it.

The hide needed another pass. Andrej leaned his weight into the scraper and let the rhythm of it fill his head, the way he had learned to fill his head with work when thinking became dangerous.


Rimmon was standing at the edge of the yard.


He had known he was there before he turned. He had learned Rimmon's particular quality of stillness early — the way other men shifted their weight, scratched at their necks, announced themselves with small restless movements. Rimmon went still when something mattered to him. It was one of the first things Andrej had catalogued, back when cataloguing such things was survival and not whatever it had quietly become.


He finished the toggle before turning. He owed the hide that much.


The scroll in Rimmon's hand told him enough. Something formal. Something Rimmon had prepared. He felt the old wariness settle into his joints like cold weather.


*Come with me.*


He processed the words the way he processed most things — slowly, from the outside in, checking each surface before trusting any weight to it. A contract. A tannery on the outskirts. Working together, Rimmon had said. Just like here.


The laugh came out before he could stop it.


Not because it was funny. Because he recognized it. The shape of it, the generous, half-true thing being offered. He had been given shapes like this before — a roof, a meal, a place at the edge of the fire. Always at the edge. Always contingent. Always wrapped in language that made the offer sound mutual when it wasn't.


He didn't say most of what he thought. He rarely did.


What he thought was: *you do not see me at all.* Not with cruelty — Rimmon was not cruel, which was perhaps the problem. He saw something he wanted to keep close and built a structure around it and called the structure generosity.


What he thought was: *I have been kept before.*


What he said was the anger, which was easier and cleaner and left less of him exposed. The anger he didn't entirely mean — or meant only on the surface, where Rimmon could receive it and carry it away with him when he left.


Because Rimmon would leave. That had always been the fixed point. Andrej had understood this since the first time he noticed Rimmon's hands on a piece of tooled leather, the particular pride in them, the future already written in the set of his shoulders. Men like Rimmon had destinations. Andrej had learned not to be someone else's destination — it never ended well for him.


The boys had been different. Jugur's loud, indiscriminate warmth. Batu and Qayan pulling him into arguments he didn't understand and didn't need to, just for the company of it. Even Darian, who was mostly noise. They asked nothing of him except to be present, which was a thing he could do. Presence cost him less than most things.


Rimmon had always cost more. He had known that and allowed it anyway, which was its own kind of foolishness.


*I do not want to lose you.*


He kept his back turned for that part. It was easier.


He scraped the hide. He listened to Rimmon's footsteps crossing the yard, leaving it, growing faint. He kept scraping long after the sound was gone.


The work continued. It always continued. That was the thing about work — it didn't ask him what he felt. It just waited for him to do it, and when he did, it was enough.


He didn't let himself think about the night in the storehouse. The pinky finger in the dark. The way the firelight had made everything feel like it existed outside of consequences.


That was the spirit talking. That was a night that didn't count.


He scraped.



Rough in places, but hopefully it gives you something to push against. I tried to keep his interiority sparse — he observes, he catalogues, he doesn't indulge. The core tension being that he understood what Rimmon felt and chose, quietly, not to receive it. Not from coldness but from a kind of self-protective realism about what he is permitted to have.
