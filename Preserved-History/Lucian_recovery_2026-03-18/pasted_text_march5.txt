The key was heavy in Andrej's hand, heavier than iron had any right to be. It was the weight of a choice, of a door opening onto a cliff edge, of a future crumbling into ash even as he reached for it.
You are a fool, he had said to Rimmon. But the greater fool stood here, his fingers wrapped around cold metal, his heart a war drum beating a funeral march in his chest.
Rimmon's confession still rang in his ears, each word a brand searing into the soft tissue of his carefully constructed defenses. You are my life. You are the only thing that has ever made me feel real.
The words should have been a gift. They were a curse.
Because knowing changed everything. Knowing meant he couldn't pretend anymore. Couldn't tell himself that the warmth in his chest when Rimmon laughed was simple gratitude. Couldn't dismiss the way his skin remembered every accidental touch as the residue of Kharshef, of a body starved for kindness. Couldn't lie to himself that the ache he felt watching Rimmon's hands work leather was admiration for a craftsman.
Knowing meant admitting that for four years, he had been in love with a man he could never have.
And Rimmon—selfish, beautiful, doomed Rimmon—had chosen tonight to tear that truth into the light. Two months before he left for silk and scribes and a wife who would bear his name. Two months before Andrej would be left behind with nothing but the ghost of what if haunting every corner of this tannery.
Knowing does not help, he had snarled. Knowing makes it a torture.
And it was torture. The cruelest kind. Because even as rage burned hot in his throat, even as he wanted to shove Rimmon away and scream at him for his selfishness, his cowardice, his timing—
Even then, he wanted.
Gods, how he wanted.
He wanted to grab Rimmon by the front of his tunic and kiss him until neither of them could breathe. He wanted to feel those careful, clumsy hands on his skin. He wanted to hear Rimmon say his name like a prayer one more time. He wanted to take this one selfish, stupid, devastating thing for himself before the world closed its fist around them both.
The key turned in the lock. The metal shrieked, too loud in the quiet night, and the door groaned open onto the familiar darkness of the tannery yard.
Andrej stepped across the threshold and stopped.
He didn't look back. If he looked back, if he saw Rimmon's face—the hope, the hunger, the desperate plea written there—he would either slam the door shut or fall to his knees. Neither was acceptable. He had one scrap of dignity left, one thin thread of control, and it was this: he would not beg. He would not ask.
He would simply open the door and let Rimmon choose.
Let him be the one to cross, Andrej thought, his hand trembling on the wood. Let him take the step into damnation. Let it be his choice, not mine dragging him down.
The lie tasted bitter even as he thought it. This was his choice too. The moment he'd pulled the key from his tunic, the moment he'd jammed it into the lock, he had chosen. He was choosing now, standing in this dark threshold, holding the door open like an invitation to his own ruin.
He heard Rimmon's footsteps. Felt the shift in the air as Rimmon moved past him, close enough that their shoulders nearly brushed. The heat of him, the smell of him—cheap spirit and cedar and salt—was overwhelming.
Andrej pushed the door shut.
The latch clicked home with a finality that echoed in his bones. Sealed in. The rest of the world locked out. Just the two of them in the thick, pungent darkness of a place that smelled of death and transformation.
Fitting, some distant part of him thought. This is a kind of death.
He turned to face Rimmon, though he could barely see him in the gloom. Just a shape, a presence, a point of heat in the dark. His hands were shaking. He clenched them into fists, nails biting into his palms.
Say it. Say the thing that will make sure he carries this forever. Don't let him escape into forgetting. Don't let him pretend this was nothing.
"If we do this," Andrej said, and his voice came out steadier than he felt, stripped clean of everything but the raw truth, "you do not get to forget. When you are in your river city, with your fine wife, you do not get to pretend this was a drunken dream. You carry it. You carry the weight of it. That is the price."
That is the price for taking this from me. For making me want it. For leaving anyway.
Rimmon's response was immediate, fierce: "I will carry it. I will carry it to my grave."
The vow settled over Andrej like a shroud. It should have been satisfying. It wasn't. It was just more weight, more inevitability, more proof that they were both damning themselves with open eyes.
And still, he wanted.
The wanting was a living thing now, clawing its way up from the place he'd buried it. Four years of careful distance, of safe friendship, of never letting his gaze linger too long—it all burned away in the space of a heartbeat. What did caution matter now? What did survival matter when the only thing that had made surviving worth it was about to be ripped away?
If I'm going to be tortured by knowing, I'm taking everything.
Andrej moved.
He didn't think. Thinking was the enemy. Thinking would remind him of consequences, of the morning, of the two months that would crawl by like years before Rimmon left forever. Thinking would tell him this was madness.
So he didn't think. He just acted.
He collided with Rimmon like a man throwing himself off a cliff, his body slamming Rimmon back against the rough wood of the gate. The impact jarred them both, but Andrej didn't pause. He captured Rimmon's mouth with his own, kissing him with all the fury and hunger and desperate, aching need he'd been choking on for four endless years.
This was nothing like the kiss outside. That had been shock, reciprocation born of drink and proximity. This was intent. This was Andrej taking what he wanted, claiming what he'd been denied, pouring every unspoken truth into the press of his lips.
Mine, something savage in him snarled. For this hour, for this night, mine.
Rimmon's hands tangled in his hair, pulling him closer, and the sound he made—a groan of pure relief—sent heat searing down Andrej's spine. He tasted like grain spirit and salt and something indefinably Rimmon, and Andrej wanted to drown in it.
His hands were everywhere, frantic and graceless. He gripped Rimmon's waist hard enough to bruise, his fingers digging in, staking a claim on flesh that would be someone else's in two months. He slid his palms up to Rimmon's neck, feeling the rapid flutter of pulse beneath his thumbs, the proof of life, of want, of shared hunger.
He broke the kiss to bury his face in the curve of Rimmon's neck, inhaling sharply. The scent of him—sweat and skin and sun-warmed linen—was intoxicating. It filled Andrej's lungs like the first breath after drowning, and he shuddered with the force of it.
"Rimmon," he gasped against the salt-slick skin. "Gods. Rimmon."
I love you. I hate you. I am ruined by you.
The words tangled in his throat, too big and too dangerous to speak. Instead, he just breathed his name like a confession, like a curse, like the only truth that mattered.
"I'm here," Rimmon whispered, and the tenderness in his voice was a knife between Andrej's ribs. "I'm right here."
For now. Not forever. Just now.
"Come," Andrej commanded, grabbing Rimmon's hand. His grip was crushing, possessive, the grip of a man holding onto the only real thing in a world of lies.
He pulled Rimmon deeper into the tannery, navigating by instinct through the familiar obstacle course of vats and racks. The moonlight filtered weakly through the roof slats, painting everything in shades of silver and shadow. This place had been his prison, his salvation, his home. Now it would be his witness.
At the back, where the finished hides were stacked, Andrej didn't hesitate. He pulled down a pile of soft goat skins, letting them tumble onto the packed earth floor in a chaotic heap. Not a bed. Not soft. Just real, just here, just theirs.
He turned to face Rimmon, his chest heaving. The moonlight carved Rimmon's face into planes of light and shadow, illuminating the dark eyes, the parted lips, the expression of awe and want that made Andrej's breath catch.
This is what I get. This is all I get. Make it count.
"No more words," Andrej said, his voice rough with need. "I am done with words."
Words were Rimmon's weapon. Words were how he'd torn Andrej apart tonight with confessions and promises he couldn't keep. Andrej didn't want words anymore. He wanted touch. He wanted proof. He wanted the reality of skin and breath and the honesty of bodies that couldn't lie.
He reached out and took hold of the hem of Rimmon's tunic, pulling it up and over his head in one motion. The fabric whispered against skin, and then Rimmon was bare from the waist up, pale in the moonlight, beautiful and offered and his.
Andrej stripped off his own tunic with shaking hands, tossing it into the darkness. And then there was nothing between them but air and want and the terrible, perfect knowledge that this was happening.
Skin to skin.
The first touch was a revelation and a devastation. Andrej pulled Rimmon down onto the pile of hides, and the sensation of Rimmon's weight on top of him, the solid warmth of him, the realness—it was almost too much. His hands mapped the geography of Rimmon's back, the muscles shifting beneath his palms, the small scars and imperfections that made him human, made him real.
Mine, he thought again, fierce and possessive. For this hour. Mine.
His hands were rough from years of tannery work, calloused and scarred, and he used them without gentleness. He touched Rimmon like he was trying to memorize him, like he was carving the shape of him into his own flesh. Every curve, every angle, every point where their bodies met and generated heat.
Rimmon's hands were just as desperate, exploring Andrej with a kind of reverent frenzy, touching his face, his chest, his stomach, as if confirming he was solid and present and willing.
"Mine," Andrej muttered against Rimmon's collarbone, the word escaping before he could stop it. "For this hour. Mine."
"Yours," Rimmon gasped, arching into his touch. "Always yours."
Liar, Andrej thought with vicious clarity. Always is two months.
But he didn't say it. He just kissed him again, hard and claiming, and let the lie stand because it was the only version of always they would ever get.
There was no finesse. No practiced seduction. Just need meeting need in the dark, two bodies learning the language of each other with urgency and desperation. Andrej had never done this before—had never wanted to, never trusted anyone enough, never felt safe enough to be this vulnerable. But with Rimmon, he didn't think about safety. He didn't think about consequences. He just felt.
The smell of the hides surrounded them, earthy and animal and real. The grit of the floor bit into his back. The salt of Rimmon's skin on his tongue was ambrosia and poison at once. Every sensation was heightened, seared into his memory with painful clarity.
Remember this, he commanded himself. Remember all of it. The weight of him. The taste of him. The sound of his breathing. This is all you get.
When the crescendo came, it shattered them both. Andrej heard himself make a sound—raw and broken and utterly beyond his control—and then he was falling, drowning, burning alive in sensation and emotion too vast to contain.
The aftermath was a slow return to gravity. Andrej lay beneath Rimmon's weight, their bodies slick with sweat and cooling in the night air. He was trembling. He couldn't stop. His arms came up to wrap around Rimmon's back, holding him there, unwilling to let go of this last moment of connection.
Rimmon's hand drifted through his hair, gentle and tender, and it was that gentleness that broke him.
The tears came hot and silent, spilling from the corners of his eyes and soaking into Rimmon's skin where Andrej's face was pressed against his throat. He didn't make a sound. He just let them fall, let the grief and the fury and the devastating, aching love pour out of him in salt water.
This is what you've done to me, he thought, the words bitter and broken in his mind. This is the price I pay for loving you. This is what knowing costs.
He had given Rimmon everything. His body, his truth, his vulnerability. And in two months, Rimmon would take all of it to Kar-Utushu and leave Andrej here with nothing but these memories and the taste of ashes.
You selfish, beautiful fool. You've ruined me. And I let you.
Rimmon's arms tightened around him, as if he could feel the tears, the grief, the silent breaking. But he didn't speak. There was nothing to say. No comfort that wouldn't be a lie. So they just lay there in the wreckage of their want, holding each other against the inevitable dawn, two ghosts haunting the place where their future had died.
And Andrej cried. For what they had. For what they'd never have. For the man who would carry his heart to another city and give his name to someone else while Andrej stayed behind in the smell of leather and lye, carrying the weight of knowing forever.
