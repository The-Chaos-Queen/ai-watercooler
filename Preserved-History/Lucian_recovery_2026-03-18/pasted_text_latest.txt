Aya - That Same Evening

---

The house was asleep. Or pretending to be. I'd learned long ago that silence in my father's house meant nothing—everyone was always listening, always weighing, always calculating the cost of what they'd overheard.

But tonight I didn't care.

I'd spent the hours since Dadaga's interruption pacing my room like a caged thing, replaying every second of that moment in the storeroom. The way Claudius had looked at me—not like property, not like a slave caught dreaming, but like a man who'd forgotten why he shouldn't want.

The way his voice had cracked on my name.

The way his body had pressed against mine for one electric heartbeat before the world crashed back in.

I wanted more.

I wanted all of it.

So when the household settled into its midnight breathing, I pulled on my plainest cloak, left my sandals by the door, and crept through shadows I'd mapped since childhood.

The slave quarters were separate from the main house. Of course they were. Out of sight, out of mind, out of the way of respectable feet.

I'd never been there before.

I'd never had reason.

---

His door was rough wood, poorly fitted, the kind of door that said nothing valuable lives here.

I knocked anyway.

Three taps. Pause. Two more.

A pattern I'd invented on the walk over, hoping he'd open before someone saw me standing here like the biggest fool in five cities.

He opened.

And everything I'd planned to say—the clever openings, the careful justifications, the layers of performance I'd wrapped myself in since I could talk—evaporated.

He stood there in the lampless dark, his eyes finding me instantly, and I remembered that he saw everything. Saw through everything. That's what made him dangerous. That's what made him mine.

"Can I come in?"

He stepped back. No questions. No hesitation. Just that silent, devastating grace that made me want to break things just to watch him react.

I slipped past him, and the door closed behind me, and suddenly we were alone in the small, bare room that smelled of him—sweat and dust and something warmer underneath, something that made my chest tight.

For a long moment, neither of us spoke. I could feel him against the door, watching me. Waiting.

"Your uncle—" he started.

"Doesn't know I'm here."

"If he finds out—"

"He won't."

I let the cloak fall. Underneath, I'd worn the plainest thing I owned—a linen shift, soft from washing, nothing that marked me as anyone's daughter or anyone's bride or anyone's anything except a girl who'd come to claim what she wanted.

His eyes moved over me once, quick as a blade, then fixed on my face.

"You shouldn't be here," he said, and his voice was rough in a way that made my stomach flip.

"I know."

"If they catch you—"

"They won't."

"Aya—"

"Claudius." I crossed the space between us in two steps, close enough now to see the pulse in his throat, close enough to smell the soap on his skin. "Do you want me to leave?"

Something flickered in his eyes. Want. Fear. Want again.

"What do you want?"

I smiled. That devastating half-smile I'd practiced in mirrors until it was perfect, until it made people nervous.

"You said you wanted me. More than anything since they took your freedom."

"I did."

"Did you mean it?"

The silence stretched. I watched him decide whether to lie, whether to protect us both from what was about to happen.

"Yes."

Another step. Now there was nothing between us at all. I could feel his heat, could see the way his jaw tightened, could count the eyelashes framing those eyes that saw too much.

"Then show me."

---

He moved so slowly I could have stopped him at any point.

That was the thing about Claudius. Even when he wanted something so badly it was written in every line of his body, he gave you room to change your mind. He gave you choice.

I hated it. I loved it. I wanted to burn it down.

His hand came up to my face, and I leaned into it before he even touched me, because I'd been starving for this, because every lesson, every argument, every moment of watching him pretend I was just another obligation had been building to this single point.

"Aya," he said quietly. "If we do this—"

"We're already doing this." I pressed his palm harder against my cheek, felt the calluses, the warmth, the realness of him. "We've been doing this since the first lesson. You've just been pretending you haven't noticed."

"I noticed."

"Good."

I tilted my face up. This time, no uncle. No interruption. No reason not to—

He kissed me.

Not gently. Not carefully. Not like I was something precious that might break.

Like I was something he needed. Like I was air and water and he'd been drowning.

I made a sound I'd never made before—surprise and satisfaction and something deeper, something that lived in my bones—and my hands were in his hair before I'd decided to move them, pulling him closer, pulling him down, pulling him into me.

Gods, he tasted like everything I shouldn't want and wanted anyway.

---

He backed me against the wall—not hard, just enough that I was pinned between rough stone and harder muscle—and I arched into him, because I'd spent my whole life being proper and measured and appropriate, and this was the opposite of all of it.

This was mine.

"Claudius—"

My name in his mouth like that, desperate and reverent, undid something in me.

He kissed my jaw, my throat, the hollow where my pulse was trying to escape. I let my head fall back, gave him everything, let him mark me however he wanted.

His lips on my skin were fire.

"Tell me to stop," he murmured.

"No."

"Aya—"

"No." I grabbed his face, pulled him back up to meet my eyes. "I've spent my whole life being told what I should want, what I should be, what's appropriate for the scribe's clever daughter. I don't want appropriate. I want you. "

Something broke in his expression. Something beautiful.

"You have me."

"Then stop asking permission and show me. "

---

So he did.

And I showed him right back.

I kissed him like I'd been thinking about it since the moment he corrected my vowel sounds without apologizing for being right. Since the moment he'd looked at me with those eyes and seen not the youngest daughter but a person.

When his hands found my waist, I gasped—not from surprise, but from the sheer relief of being touched exactly where I'd imagined.

When they slid higher, tracing ribs and linen and the shape of wanting, I made a sound I couldn't have stopped if I'd tried.

"More," I whispered.

"Aya—"

"More. "

My fingers found the ties of his tunic. They were clumsy—I was clumsy, because I'd never done this, never wanted anyone enough to risk it—but I didn't care. I needed him bare. I needed skin.

"We shouldn't—" he started, even as his arms tightened around me.

"I don't care."

"If they find out—"

"Let them." His chest was warm under my palms, his skin smooth over hard muscle, and I forgot how to breathe. "Let the whole city know. Let my father know. I choose you. "

He caught my wrists. Gently, but firm.

"This isn't just about tonight. You understand that?"

"I understand."

"I can't offer you anything. No future. No protection. Not even my freedom."

"I'm not asking you to."

"Then what are you asking?"

I looked at him—this man who'd lost everything and still found ways to be kind, to be careful, to give choices even when none had been given to him—and smiled.

"Just this. Just you. Just real. "

---

He let go of my wrists.

I reached for him again, and this time there was no hesitation in either of us.

I pulled his tunic over his head and dropped it on the floor. The lamplight from the single high window caught his skin, traced the muscles of his chest, the scars I hadn't known he carried.

I touched them. Lightly. Questioning.

He flinched—not away, just a small reflexive movement, as if he'd forgotten they were there.

"Who gave you these?"

"No one who matters anymore."

I kissed the longest one, a pale line across his ribs.

"Aya—"

I kissed another. Higher, closer to his heart.

"You're shaking," I murmured against his skin.

"You're dangerous."

"Good." I looked up at him, this impossible man who'd walked out of a burning city and into my life. "I want to be dangerous for you."

He kissed me then—fierce and deep and hungry—and I kissed him back with everything I had.

We moved to his pallet somehow. I don't remember walking. I remember falling together, skin to skin, breath to breath, the world outside this room burning away until there was nothing left but us.

His hands mapped me like I was a text he'd been studying his whole life. Every place he touched came alive, sparking, needing more.

My hands mapped him right back. The width of his shoulders, the strength of his back, the way his muscles jumped under my fingers like he wasn't used to being touched with tenderness.

"Aya." His voice was wrecked. "Aya, I—"

"I know." I pulled his face down to mine. "I know."

---

Later—minutes or hours, I couldn't say—we lay tangled together on his narrow pallet, his arm around me, my head on his chest, the slow rhythm of his heartbeat under my ear.

The high window showed a slice of star-scattered sky.

"What happens now?" I asked.

"Now?" His hand traced slow circles on my shoulder. "Now you sneak back to your room and pretend this never happened."

"That's not what I meant."

"I know."

I propped myself up on one elbow to look at him. In the dim light, his face was all shadows and angles, beautiful and sad.

"I'm not pretending this didn't happen."

"Good."

"I'm not pretending I don't want it to happen again."

"Aya—"

"I'm not pretending anything, Claudius. Not anymore." I touched his face, traced the line of his jaw. "You're the only real thing in my life. Everything else is performance. Everything else is what they expect. But you—" I kissed him, soft and slow. "You're just you. And I want you. All of you. However long we have."

Something flickered in his eyes. Hope, maybe. Or fear of hope.

"You can't say things like that."

"Why not?"

"Because I'll start believing them."

I smiled against his mouth.

"Good."

---

I left before dawn, the way I'd come—silent as a ghost, wrapped in my plain cloak, carrying the memory of his skin on mine like a secret too precious to share.

Back in my room, I pressed my face into my pillow and smiled.

Tomorrow, I'd be the scribe's clever daughter again. I'd argue with Silim about grammar and dodge Bēlessunu's questions about why I looked so tired and pretend that everything was exactly as it should be.

But tonight—tonight was mine.

Tonight was real.

And tomorrow, when Claudius walked into the lesson room and met my eyes across the clay tablets, he'd know exactly what I was thinking.

You're mine.

I don't care who knows it.

I choose you.

Every single time.
