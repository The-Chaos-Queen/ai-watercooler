## Claude

Great question! The terms get used inconsistently, but here are the two main definitions:

**Definition 1: Magic Saturation (most common)**

**High Fantasy:**

- Magic is common, known, and integrated into society
- Multiple magical species/races (elves, dragons, etc.)
- Often secondary worlds (not Earth)
- Examples: *Lord of the Rings*, *Wheel of Time*, *Stormlight Archive*

**Low Fantasy:**

- Magic is rare, subtle, or ambiguous
- Mostly human cast
- Can be set in our world or a secondary world
- Examples: *A Song of Ice and Fire* (early books), *The First Law*, *The Lies of Locke Lamora*

**Definition 2: World Type (older usage)**

**High Fantasy:** Set in a completely secondary world (not Earth)
**Low Fantasy:** Set in our world, or our world with magical elements added

---

**Where your story sits:**

By Definition 1: **Low fantasy** - Magic exists (the Silent One, possibly Andrej's resilience) but it's rare, mysterious, and not systematized. It's a human-dominated world where magic is the exception.

By Definition 2: **High fantasy** - It's a secondary world (Seven Oases, fictional cultures and geography).

Most people use Definition 1 these days, so I'd call your book **low fantasy** or **dark fantasy** depending on how much you want to emphasize the horror elements (Nasqu's collection, the violence, the bleakness).

Does that help with positioning?
