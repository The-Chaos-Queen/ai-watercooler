## Findings
- In a full-evaluator two-row canary, the first resolver callback replaced
  `binding.resolve`. The replacement alone handled the second row, which became
  `resolved` and `GROWTH` under the original resolver identity even though the
  original callable would reject it.
- A whitespace resolver identifier is published while its receipt records empty
  identity and a binding-validity error.
- A wrong-typed identifier is silently converted to `resolver=None`, producing
  an `unbound` receipt with no resolver-specific incomplete reason.
- The public helper still returns `{}` for a non-exact outer container, row
  subclass, and malformed evidence-type leaf.
- Accepted canaries confirmed identity plus version remain original after one
  callback and a hostile non-exact binding receives zero getter reads.
- The declared suite returned 159 passed; focused Ruff, diff check, and pinned
  blobs were clean.

## Risks / Watch Out For
- Stable resolver identity does not establish stable authority if the callable
  is retrieved again after a callback can mutate its source object.
- Translating malformed-bound to unbound falsifies the decision artifact even
  when the outcome remains fail-closed.
- Passing the declared suite does not clear external calibration or downstream
  launch holds.
