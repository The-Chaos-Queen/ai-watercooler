# **A hybrid model based on** **transformer and Mamba for** **enhanced sequence modeling**

**Xiaocui Zhu** **[1]** [] **, Qunsheng Ruan** **[2,3]** **, Sai Qian** **[1,3]** **& Miaohui Zhang** **[1,3]**


**State Space Models (SSMs) have made remarkable strides in language modeling in recent years.**
**With the introduction of Mamba, these models have garnered increased attention, often surpassing**
**Transformers in specific areas. Nevertheless, despite Mamba’s unique strengths, Transformers remain**
**essential due to their advanced computational capabilities and proven effectiveness. In this paper,**
**we propose a novel model that effectively integrates the strengths of both Transformers and Mamba.**
**Specifically, our model utilizes the Transformer’s encoder for encoding tasks while employing Mamba**
**as the decoder for decoding tasks. We introduce a feature fusion technique that combines the features**
**generated by the encoder with the hidden states produced by the decoder. This approach successfully**
**merges the advantages of the Transformer and Mamba, resulting in enhanced performance.**
**Comprehensive experiments across various language tasks demonstrate that our proposed model**
**consistently achieves competitive results, outperforming existing benchmarks.**


**Keywords** State space models (SSMs), Transformer, Mamba, Feature fusion



The evolution of neural network architectures for sequence modeling has witnessed substantial advancements
over the past few decades. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), introduced by [1], established foundational concepts
in natural language processing (NLP) through their ability to maintain hidden states and capture temporal
dependencies. However, RNNs are limited by vanishing and exploding gradients, which impede their capacity
to learn long-range dependencies. To mitigate these challenges, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) Networks,
proposed by [2], and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs), introduced by [2], incorporated gating mechanisms to enhance
the flow of information. Despite these variants’ performance improvements, their inherently sequential nature
restricted parallelization, thus limiting efficiency and scalability.

3introduction of the Transformer model in 2017 represented a significant breakthrough in sequence modeling.
By utilizing self-attention mechanisms, Transformers eliminated the necessity for recurrent connections, thereby
enabling the efficient capture of long-range dependencies. The parallelizable architecture of Transformers
facilitated the training of significantly larger models on extensive datasets, resulting in unparalleled performance
in various NLP tasks. Nonetheless, researchers continued to investigate alternative approaches to sequence
modeling. State Space Models (SSMs), as outlined by [4], emerged as a promising avenue, employing continuoustime dynamics for sequence representation. SSMs offer a unique perspective by modeling sequences through
latent space state transitions, effectively capturing intricate temporal patterns.

The introduction of Mamba, developed by [5] and further elaborated by [6], advanced the capabilities of SSMs.
Mamba integrates the principles of state space modeling with innovative neural architecture design, providing a
robust framework for sequence modeling. By addressing the limitations associated with RNNs and Transformers,
Mamba demonstrated enhanced performance across a range of language tasks, exceeding traditional models in
accuracy and efficiency. While Mamba exhibits greater efficiency compared to Transformers and demonstrates
enhanced power relative to conventional SSMs, it is also noted that Mamba may need to match the efficiency of
SSMs in specific tasks or the power of Transformers in others. Consequently, a sensible approach is to explore the
combination of Mamba with Transformers, as illustrated in Fig. 1. Effectively integrating the strengths of both
Transformers and Mamba has emerged as a promising research avenue.

This paper presents a novel hybrid model that amalgamates the strengths of both Transformers and Mamba.
We leverage the pre-trained Transformer’s encoder to encode the target while employing Mamba as the decoder.
Utilizing the Transformer’s encoder within the Mamba framework capitalizes on the advantages of both
architectures, leading to enhanced feature representations, improved parallelization, and greater adaptability

**Related works**
**Transformer**
The introduction of Transformer models by [3] marks a revolutionary development in natural language processing
(NLP). The original Transformer architecture relies exclusively on self-attention mechanisms to capture
dependencies between words within a sequence, establishing new benchmarks across various NLP tasks,
including machine translation [7][,][8], summarization [9][,][10], and sentiment analysis [11][,][12] .

The success of the Transformer model has prompted the development of various variants and extensions.
Notably, [13] introduced BERT in 2018, employing a bidirectional training approach focused on masked language
modeling tasks, significantly enhancing the state-of-the-art in numerous NLP benchmarks. Following BERT,
models such as GPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer) by [14] from OpenAI have exemplified the efficacy of
Transformer-based architectures in generating coherent and contextually relevant text further expanding the
potential of NLP models. Another crucial development area is the application of Transformers to multimodal
tasks and cross-lingual modeling. Models, such as Vision Transformer (ViT) by [15], have adapted the Transformer
architecture for image classification, while multilingual versions of BERT, including XLM-R by [16], have
demonstrated remarkable performance in cross-lingual understanding tasks.


**Mamba**
The Mamba model signifies a notable advancement in state space modeling (SSM) for sequence processing
tasks. Traditionally, state space models have effectively captured temporal dependencies and the behaviors of
dynamical systems; however, their application within large-scale and complex NLP tasks has been constrained
by computational efficiency and model complexity issues. Recent endeavors to enhance SSMs have yielded
innovative architectures combining principles from classical SSMs and contemporary neural network designs.
The introduction of the S4 (Structured State Space for Sequence Modeling) model by [17] represented a pivotal
advancement, showcasing how structured state spaces can achieve superior performance in sequence modeling
tasks. Building upon this framework, the Mamba model has been developed to optimize further and broaden
the capabilities of state space models. Mamba integrates the dynamical systems approach of SSMs with neural
network techniques, thereby augmenting its performance across NLP tasks. The encouraging outcomes of
Mamba models in recent benchmarks underscore their potential to complement and, in certain instances,

**Hybrid model**
The advancement of Mamba models has generated considerable interest in the potential of hybrid architectures
that can exploit the strengths of both state space models and neural network structures. The Jamba model
proposed by [22] interleaves blocks of Transformer and Mamba layers, thereby harnessing the advantages of both
model families. In some of these layers, a mixture of experts (MoE) is introduced to enhance model capacity while
maintaining manageable active parameter usage. [23] introduce a hybrid structure termed Block-State Transformer
(BST), which integrates an SSM sublayer for long-range contextualization with a Block Transformer sublayer for
short-term representation of sequences. These advancements highlight the promise of hybrid models, providing
a versatile and powerful framework capable of leveraging the complementary strengths of various architectures.



**Feature fusion**
Feature fusion constitutes a critical area of research within natural language processing (NLP) and multimodal
learning, with significant progress stemming from developing attention mechanisms and integrating
convolutional neural networks (CNNs) alongside Transformers. In NLP tasks, the attention mechanism,
particularly the multi-head attention within the Transformer decoder, serves as a quintessential structure for
feature fusion alongside cross-attention mechanisms applied to vision tasks [24] . Both mechanisms proficiently
facilitate feature integration. As multimodal tasks gain prominence, the fusion of features between Transformers
and convolutional layers has become increasingly important, resulting in numerous related advancements [25][–][27] .

Overall, the evolution of feature fusion techniques from attention mechanisms in NLP to the integration
of CNNs and Transformers in multimodal learning highlights the continuous innovation aimed at improving
model performance. These advancements underscore the importance of effectively combining features from
different sources to create richer and more informative representations, driving further progress in both NLP
and multimodal applications.

**Analyze**
To support the specific combination of Transformer and Mamba models, we analyzed their individual strengths
in sequence modeling tasks, particularly focusing on how their unique features complement each other.

The Transformer model is highly regarded for its parallel processing capability and effective contextual capture
through the multi-head self-attention mechanism. This feature allows it to capture long-range dependencies and
perform complex syntactic analyses without relying on a fixed sequence order, making it particularly suitable
for handling lengthy texts. However, Transformers can struggle to maintain local sequential coherence due to
their design, which does not inherently prioritize temporal dependencies. In contrast, the Mamba model excels
in modeling sequence order and temporal dependencies, processing sequences step-by-step. This design is
advantageous in tasks requiring strong sequential consistency and dependency modeling. Mamba’s architecture
inherently captures local sequential dependencies, allowing it to process information with greater temporal
alignment, which is essential in tasks with strong order dependencies.

The integration of Transformer and Mamba leverages their complementary strengths: the Transformer
encoder provides global feature extraction, capturing the text’s long-range dependencies and contextual
information, while the Mamba decoder sequentially models the data to reinforce local dependencies. This
complementary feature alignment enhances the model’s semantic understanding in long-sequence tasks by
combining the Transformers’ global contextualization with Mamba’s local sequential modeling. By combining
the Transformers’ global attention with Mamba’s localized sequential modeling, this hybrid model achieves
a more comprehensive semantic representation. The integration surpasses the performance of standalone
Transformer or Mamba models in complex natural language processing tasks, providing richer and more robust
representations.

This chapter introduces the TransMamba model, which combines the Transformer encoder and Mamba
decoder to enhance sequence modeling for natural language tasks. The model fuses features generated by the
Transformer with hidden states from Mamba, processed through a feature fusion mechanism. This integration
preserves the parallel training capabilities of State Space Models (SSMs), ensuring efficient and scalable training.
The TransMamba architecture effectively leverages the strengths of both components, resulting in superior
performance and generalization across various applications.

**Quantitative analysis**
Based on the experimental results presented in Table 2, it is evident that the proposed TransMamba model
outperforms both the Mamba and Transformer models across a variety of tasks.

In the zero-shot evaluation setting, TransMamba consistently achieves higher scores than the baseline models.
Specifically, TransMamba demonstrates superior performance on HellaSwag, BoolQ, StoryCloze, ARC-easy,
and ARCchallenge, with average accuracy improvements. For instance, TransMamba achieves an accuracy of
35.13 on HellaSwag, surpassing the Transformer (33.19) and Mamba (34.89). Similarly, TransMamba shows an
impressive score on BoolQ with 60.99, outperforming both Transformer (59.27) and Mamba (55.78). Similarly,
on the BoolQ data set, TransMamba has also achieved remarkable results with an accuracy rate of 60.99%, Better
than the Transformer 59.27% And those found in Mamba’s case 55.78%.

In the few-shot setting, TransMamba continues to exhibit competitive performance. It achieves the highest
scores on StoryCloze (60.64), SciQ (81.7), and ARC-easy (59.48). While the Transformer model slightly
surpasses TransMamba on BoolQ in the few-shot scenario, TransMamba still shows a significant improvement
in the overall average score.

These results highlight the effectiveness of the hybrid approach in integrating the strengths of both
Transformers and Mamba. The superior performance of TransMamba can be attributed to its ability to leverage
the rich feature representations generated by the Transformer encoder and the powerful sequential modeling
capabilities of the Mamba decoder. This fusion of features allows the model to capture a broader range of
information and dependencies, resulting in enhanced accuracy and generalization across diverse natural
language processing tasks. The findings underscore the potential of TransMamba as a robust and efficient
solution for complex sequence modeling challenges.

The integration of the Transformer and Mamba models significantly enhances the learning dynamics of the
TransMamba architecture. By leveraging the strengths of both models, the hybrid approach allows for improved
representation learning, capturing long-range dependencies through the Transformer’s self-attention mechanism
while maintaining local sequential coherence with Mamba. The results indicate that this combination not only
leads to richer feature representations but also improves training stability, effectively mitigating issues such as
gradient instability that can occur in deep networks.

In terms of computational efficiency, the findings suggest that while combining two distinct architectures
may introduce additional demands, the overall efficiency of TransMamba remains competitive. The parallel
processing capabilities of the Transformer complement Mamba’s sequential operations, resulting in balanced
resource utilization. Furthermore, TransMamba can achieve competitive inference speeds and maintains its
effectiveness even with increased dataset sizes, showcasing strong scalability.

Overall, the results demonstrate that TransMamba is adaptable across various tasks, performing well
across multiple benchmarks. This adaptability highlights the model’s robustness and suitability for real-world
applications, reaffirming the value of integrating the Transformer and Mamba models in complex natural
language processing tasks.

**Conclusion**
We propose TransMamba, a novel hybrid model that effectively integrates the strengths of Transformers and
Mamba for sequence modeling tasks. The model leverages the pre-trained Transformer encoder for encoding, while
utilizing Mamba as the decoder, thereby capitalizing on the advantages of both architectures. The Transformer
excels in generating rich feature representations and enabling parallelization, while Mamba contributes to
improved efficiency and modeling capability. The Transformer provides powerful feature representations and
parallelization capabilities, while Mamba enhances efficiency and modeling power. Additionally, the authors
propose a feature fusion technique to integrate the features generated by the Transformer encoder with the
hidden states produced during the decoding process by Mamba, allowing the model to capture a broader
range of information for improved performance. Experimental results across several language tasks, including
commonsense reasoning, question answering, and science question answering, demonstrate that the TransMamba
model consistently outperforms existing benchmarks. The hybrid approach validates the synergistic effect of
combining Transformer and Mamba components, showing enhanced performance across tasks. These results
highlight TransMamba as a promising solution for sequence modeling, offering improvements over traditional
models. However, some areas of the article could benefit from further exploration. For instance, while the model
shows promising results, there is a lack of detailed performance comparison with other state-of-the-art models,
which would help provide a clearer picture of its relative strengths. Additionally, the article does not address the
computational complexity and training/inference time of the model, which are important factors for practical
implementation and deployment. Lastly, while the feature fusion technique is an integral part of the model, a
more thorough discussion on its specific contribution to performance would enhance the understanding of its
effectiveness. Despite these limitations, the TransMamba model presents a significant advancement in hybrid
sequence modeling, offering valuable insights and potential for further refinement.
