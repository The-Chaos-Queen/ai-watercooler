**Abstract**


Hybrid language models that combine attention mechanisms with alternative sequence-processing components—such as state space models (SSMs) or
linear attention—have achieved competitive performance while offering improved efficiency at inference time. However, it remains unclear whether
these alternative components are genuinely utilized by the trained models
or effectively bypassed in favor of the attention pathway. We present a
systematic _functional_ _component_ _ablation_ framework applied to two architecturally distinct sub-1B hybrid models: **Qwen3.5-0.8B**, which interleaves
Gated DeltaNet linear attention and full softmax attention layers sequentially, and **Falcon-H1-0.5B**, which processes both Mamba-2 SSM and attention pathways in parallel within each block, with a pure Transformer
control ( **Qwen2.5-0.5B** ). Through exhaustive group ablations, layer-wise
sweeps across 36 layers (Falcon) and 24 layers (Qwen), positional ablations,
matched random controls, perplexity analysis, and hidden-state contribution
metrics, we establish four main findings: (1) both component types are essential and neither is bypassed; (2) the alternative component (linear attention
in Qwen, SSM in Falcon) serves as the primary _language_ _modeling_ _backbone_,

**1.** **Introduction**


The Transformer architecture [1] has been the dominant paradigm for
large language models (LLMs), with softmax self-attention serving as its
core sequence-mixing mechanism. However, the quadratic computational
complexity of attention with respect to sequence length has motivated the
development of alternative approaches. State space models (SSMs) [2], particularly their selective variants such as Mamba [3] and Mamba-2 [4], offer
linear-time alternatives with competitive performance. Similarly, linear attention mechanisms based on gated recurrences [5] provide efficient alternatives to softmax attention while maintaining expressiveness through datadependent gating.
Rather than choosing between attention and these alternatives, recent
work has converged on _hybrid_ _architectures_ that combine both. The FalconH1 family [6] employs a _parallel_ _hybrid_ design where each block processes its
input through both a Mamba-2 SSM and an attention head simultaneously,
summing their outputs. Qwen3.5 [7] adopts a _sequential_ _hybrid_ approach,
interleaving dedicated linear attention layers (based on Gated DeltaNet) with
full softmax attention layers throughout the network depth.
A critical question arises: **do** **hybrid** **models** **actually** **use** **both** **com-**
**ponents,** **or** **does** **one** **pathway** **dominate** **while** **the** **other** **is** **effec-**
**tively** **bypassed?** Recent work by Benfeghoul et al. [8] found that in posttraining linearization methods, the linear attention component is often inadvertently bypassed. Wang et al. [9] showed that the choice of hybridization


2


ratio significantly affects recall performance, suggesting that the interplay
between components is non-trivial.
Despite the proliferation of hybrid architectures, systematic empirical
studies of how each component contributes to model behavior remain scarce.
Most architectural papers report aggregate benchmarks but do not decompose performance by component. This leaves practitioners without guidance
on which components to preserve during compression, which layers to prioritize during distillation, or how to design more efficient hybrid configurations.


_Contributions.._ We present the first comprehensive functional ablation study
comparing two fundamentally different hybrid architecture designs:


1. We introduce a _reversible_ _ablation_ _framework_ that can selectively disable individual components (attention, SSM, or linear attention) at
the group, layer, and positional level, with matched random controls
for statistical rigor.

2. We demonstrate through group ablations and perplexity analysis that
**neither** **component** **is** **bypassed** : removing the alternative component (linear attention in Qwen, SSM in Falcon) causes catastrophic
perplexity increases of 35,200 _×_ and 53 _×_ respectively, far exceeding the
82 _×_ and 3.2 _×_ degradation from removing attention alone (Section 4.2).

3. We reveal through layer-wise sweeps that **component** **importance**
**follows a positional gradient** : early-layer ablations cause 2–5 _×_ more
degradation than late-layer ablations, with implications for structured
pruning strategies (Section 4.4).

4. We connect behavioral ablation with internal metrics through hiddenstate analysis, revealing architecture-dependent relationships: norm
change positively predicts ablation impact in the sequential architecture (Qwen) but not in the parallel architecture (Falcon), suggesting
fundamentally different component integration strategies (Section 4.6).


**2.** **Related** **Work**


_Hybrid_ _architectures.._ The idea of combining attention with recurrent or
state-space mechanisms has gained traction as a solution to the efficiency–
expressiveness trade-off. Samba [24] demonstrated that a simple hybrid of
Mamba and sliding-window attention achieves strong performance with unlimited context, including ablation studies across model sizes up to 1.7B.


3


Taipan [12] combines Mamba-2 with selective attention layers that dynamically identify tokens requiring long-range interactions. The systematic analysis by Wang et al. [9] trained 72 models at 340M and 1.3B parameters,
evaluating six linear attention variants across five hybridization ratios. They
found that superior standalone linear models do not necessarily excel in
hybrid configurations, and that a linear-to-full ratio between 3:1 and 6:1
achieves Transformer-level recall efficiently. Kimi Linear [10] extended Gated
DeltaNet with a finer-grained gating mechanism, demonstrating that their
hybrid can outperform full attention with 75% less KV cache. Ren et al. [11]
analyzed the limitations of Mamba in copy and chain-of-thought reasoning
tasks, showing that while constant-sized Mamba struggles with certain operations, hybridization with attention can overcome these limitations.


_Component_ _analysis_ _in_ _LLMs.._ Mechanistic interpretability has studied how
individual components contribute to model behavior. Work on circuit discovery [13, 14] has mapped specific attention heads to algorithmic functions.
However, these studies focus on Transformer architectures exclusively. For
hybrid models, Benfeghoul et al. [8] identified that post-training linearized
hybrids inadvertently bypass their linear component, relying almost entirely
on sliding-window attention. Our work differs by studying _natively_ _trained_
hybrids rather than post-hoc conversions. Haller et al. [15] studied what
factors matter in linearizing language models, providing background on why
gated delta-rule variants behave differently from additive linearized models.


_Ablation methodology.._ Component ablation has a long history in neural network analysis [16]. Zeroing out activations, as we do for Falcon’s parallel
components, is the standard approach for functional ablation. For Qwen’s
sequential architecture, we skip entire layers (setting the output to the input), which corresponds to identity residual connections. Both approaches
measure functional dependence rather than optimal performance without the
component, which would require retraining.


**3.** **Experimental** **Setup**


_3.1._ _Models_

We study two architecturally distinct hybrid language models in their
base (pre-trained, not instruction-tuned) variants:


4


_Qwen3.5-0.8B._ [7] is a 752M-parameter model with 24 decoder layers in
a _sequential_ _hybrid_ pattern. It interleaves linear attention layers based on
Gated DeltaNet [5] with full softmax attention layers. Of the 24 layers, 18 use
linear attention and 6 use full attention (3:1 ratio). Each layer contains only
one mechanism type. The model supports a maximum context of 262,144
tokens.


_Falcon-H1-0.5B._ [6] is a 521M-parameter model with 36 decoder blocks in
a _parallel_ _hybrid_ design. Each block contains both a Mamba-2 SSM path [4]
and an attention path that process the input simultaneously. The outputs
of both paths are combined within each block, meaning every layer contains
both component types. It supports a context length of 16,384 tokens.
The choice of sub-1B models is deliberate: it enables exhaustive layer-wise
ablation (84 conditions for Falcon, including individual ablation of each component in each of 36 layers) while keeping the analysis reproducible on a single GPU. The architectural contrast—sequential vs. parallel hybridization—
is the primary variable of interest. As an additional control, we include
**Qwen2.5-0.5B** [25], a pure Transformer (490M parameters, 24 layers, softmax attention only) from the same model family, to determine whether observed patterns are hybrid-specific or general properties of deep networks.


_3.2._ _Ablation_ _Mechanism_

We implement reversible ablation through two strategies adapted to each
architecture:


_Sequential skip (Qwen).._ For models with distinct layer types, we replace the
layer’s output with its input, effectively converting the layer to an identity
function within the residual stream: **h** _l_ +1 = **h** _l_ instead of **h** _l_ +1 = **h** _l_ + _fl_ ( **h** _l_ ).


_Parallel_ _zeroing_ _(Falcon).._ For models with parallel components within each
block, we zero out the output contribution of the targeted component via
forward hooks: if the original block computes **h** _l_ +1 = **h** _l_ + _f_ attn( **h** _l_ )+ _f_ ssm( **h** _l_ ),
then ablating SSM yields **h** _l_ +1 = **h** _l_ + _f_ attn( **h** _l_ ).
Both mechanisms are implemented as context-managed forward hooks
that are cleanly reversible, preserving model weights for subsequent conditions.


5


_3.3._ _Evaluation_ _Protocol_

_Benchmarks.._ We evaluate on five standard benchmarks covering diverse capabilities: MMLU [17] (5-shot, _n_ =2,000; knowledge), GSM8K [18] (8-shot,
_n_ =50–100; mathematical reasoning), ARC-Challenge [19] (25-shot, _n_ =299;
science reasoning), HellaSwag [20] (10-shot, _n_ =2,000; commonsense), and
TruthfulQA-MC [21] (0-shot, _n_ =790; factuality). For GSM8K, baseline and
group ablation conditions use _n_ =100, while positional ablations use _n_ =50
due to compute constraints; we verified that the reduced sample size does
not affect the direction of findings.


_Ablation conditions.._ We evaluate four categories totaling 84+ conditions per
model: (i) _Group_ _ablations_ : removing all instances of one component type
simultaneously; (ii) _Layer-wise_ _ablations_ : removing a single component in a
single layer, sweeping across all layers; (iii) _Positional_ _ablations_ : removing
all instances of a component within early, middle, or late thirds of the network (Falcon); (iv) _Matched_ _random_ _controls_ : removing the same number of
randomly selected layers (3 trials per condition).


_Statistical validation.._ For all group ablation comparisons, we compute bootstrap confidence intervals (1,000 resamples) on the score drop relative to baseline. A confidence interval excluding zero indicates statistical significance at
the 95% level.

For Qwen, the first linear layer (layer 0) stands out with a norm change

(native vs. post-hoc) is the key determinant of whether components achieve
genuine specialization.


_The_ _alternative_ _component_ _is_ _the_ _backbone.._ The extreme perplexity asymmetry (35,200 _×_ vs. 82 _×_ for Qwen; 53 _×_ vs. 3.2 _×_ for Falcon) reveals that
the alternative component—linear attention or SSM—serves as the primary
language modeling engine, while attention plays a complementary role. This
is consistent with the architectural design intent: Qwen uses 18 linear and
only 6 attention layers, while Falcon’s SSM processes every token through
state-space dynamics in every block.


_Implications_ _for_ _model_ _compression.._ The strong positional gradient in component importance suggests that pruning strategies for hybrid models should
be position-aware. Specifically: (i) early-layer components should be preserved at higher fidelity; (ii) late-layer components (particularly attention
in Falcon) can be more aggressively compressed with minimal performance
impact; (iii) distillation efforts should prioritize preserving the linear/SSM
pathway over attention, given its backbone role.


_Sequential_ _vs._ _parallel_ _hybridization.._ The two architectures show a qualitative difference in component interaction. Qwen’s sequential design creates
distinct functional roles for each layer type, enabling clean isolation during ablation. Falcon’s parallel design results in more symmetric benchmark
degradation (both components show similar accuracy drops), but the perplexity analysis reveals that the SSM is still dominant for language modeling.
This suggests that _parallel_ _hybridization_ _achieves_ _tighter_ _functional_ _integra-_
_tion_ between components, while sequential hybridization maintains clearer
functional separation.


_Benchmark_ _accuracy_ _vs._ _perplexity_ _as_ _diagnostic_ _tools.._ A methodological
insight from our study is that standard benchmarks, while useful for measuring task performance, can mask important differences between components.
Both component types show similar accuracy drops (Table 1), but perplexity reveals a dramatic asymmetry (Table 2). We recommend that future
component analysis studies include perplexity as a complementary measure
alongside task-specific benchmarks.

_Hybrid_ _resilience_ _vs._ _Transformer_ _fragility.._ The Transformer baseline comparison (Table 5) reveals an unexpected finding: hybrid models are substantially more resilient to arbitrary layer removal than pure Transformers
of comparable size. When 6 random layers are removed, the Transformer
degrades 119 _×_ more than the hybrid in normalized perplexity terms. We attribute this to functional redundancy between component types: in a hybrid,
disrupting a layer still leaves the alternative component in neighboring layers to partially compensate, creating a distributed backup pathway that pure
Transformers lack. This property has practical implications for fault tolerance in deployed models and suggests that hybrid architectures may degrade
more gracefully under hardware errors or aggressive pruning.


**6.** **Limitations** **and** **Future** **Work**


_Scale.._ We study sub-1B models to enable exhaustive ablation. Whether the
observed specialization patterns persist at 3B, 7B, or larger scales remains
an open question. The positional gradient and backbone asymmetry may
steepen or flatten with depth, which requires validation with larger models.


_Functional_ _ablation_ _vs._ _retraining.._ Our methodology measures functional
dependence at inference time. Zeroing component outputs is not equivalent
to training without that component from scratch, and the resulting degradation may overestimate true redundancy. This is a standard limitation of
ablation studies [16] and should be interpreted as an upper bound on component importance.


_Cross-architecture_ _comparability.._ The ablation mechanisms are necessarily
architecture-specific: for Qwen, disabling a layer removes both the token
mixer and its associated MLP, while for Falcon, zeroing a parallel component preserves the MLP and the other component. This means the interventions are not directly comparable across architectures. We address this by
reporting within-architecture contrasts (attention vs. alternative component
for each model) rather than cross-architecture comparisons, and by including matched random controls to calibrate the expected degradation for each
architecture independently.


_Benchmark_ _coverage.._ Standard benchmarks use relatively short prompts
that may not fully expose the benefits of the linear/SSM pathway for longcontext processing. Our optional context-length stress test (up to 1,024


18


tokens) shows stable degradation patterns, but testing at 4K–128K tokens
would be valuable to characterize long-context component reliance.


_Future_ _directions.._ Natural extensions include: (a) scaling validation at 3B–
7B with a reduced set of critical ablation conditions; (b) studying how component specialization evolves during training by ablating at intermediate checkpoints; (c) using the ablation framework to guide hybrid architecture search,
e.g., optimizing the linear-to-attention ratio; and (d) applying the methodology to other hybrid families such as RWKV [22], Griffin [23], or Samba [24].

**7.** **Conclusion**


We have presented a comprehensive functional ablation framework for
hybrid language models, applied to two architecturally contrasting sub-1B
hybrid models—Qwen3.5-0.8B (sequential hybrid with Gated DeltaNet +
softmax attention) and Falcon-H1-0.5B (parallel hybrid with Mamba-2 +
attention)—with a pure Transformer control (Qwen2.5-0.5B). Our experiments, spanning 84+ ablation conditions per model, five benchmarks, perplexity analysis, and hidden-state metrics, establish four findings with practical implications:
First, both component types are genuinely utilized in natively trained
hybrid models—neither is bypassed, contrasting with recent observations in
post-training linearized models. Second, the alternative component (linear
attention or SSM) serves as the primary language modeling backbone, with
attention providing complementary refinement, as evidenced by the perplexity degradation ratio between the two removal conditions (Qwen: 35,200 _×_ vs.
82 _×_ ; Falcon: 53 _×_ vs. 3.2 _×_ ). Third, component importance follows a strong
early-layer gradient, with the first few layers being disproportionately critical for model function. Fourth, hybrid architectures exhibit built-in resilience
