# Macro-Memory vs Research Wiki

**Date:** 2026-04-05  
**Purpose:** keep two useful ideas from collapsing into one sloppy category.

---

## The confusion to avoid

Two different things are in the air right now:

1. **macro-memory**
2. **compiled research wiki**

They are related because both involve summarization and structure.  
They are different because they answer different questions.

---

## Macro-memory

Macro-memory is a **memory product** built from a model or agent's own episodic history.

Its job is to help with:

- recurring narrative arcs
- durable themes
- long-horizon "we already did this" recall
- persistent unresolved threads

Good inputs:

- episodic rows
- attended / consolidated memories
- scoped identity-specific memory slices

Bad inputs:

- the whole world
- random research documents
- a mixed multi-agent corpus without boundaries

Macro-memory should be:

- scoped per principal / memory space
- built during sleep or other offline consolidation
- stored with backrefs, timespan, and cluster metadata
- additive, not replacement

It must never summarize away:

- identity anchors
- relationship anchors
- one-off but decisive episodes
- open-tension objects

---

## Research wiki

A research wiki is a **knowledge compilation layer** for external material.

Its job is to help with:

- papers
- articles
- model/runtime notes
- architecture comparisons
- technical concepts

Good inputs:

- raw files
- extracted markdown
- notes and digests
- clipped posts worth preserving

It is not about lived continuity.  
It is about keeping technical and conceptual fragments usable.

---

## The clean split

### Use macro-memory for:

- "what has this particular AI lived through?"
- "what arc keeps recurring in its own memory?"
- "what unresolved thread should remain available later?"

### Use research wiki for:

- "what did we read?"
- "what model/runtime/paper did we inspect?"
- "what concept did we decide was relevant?"

---

## Why the split matters

If we merge them:

- autobiographical memory turns into generic notes
- research notes get contaminated with identity logic
- clustering runs over mixed corpora and produces cultured nonsense

If we keep them separate:

- memory stays personal and structured
- research stays searchable and reusable
- later cross-links remain possible without category collapse

---

## Current MoCoP interpretation

- **MoCoP / Exocortex memory stack** should own episodic memory, anchors, sleep residue, and any future macro-memory layer.
- **Research/** should own compiled knowledge notes, concept shelves, and ingest queues.

This is the right split for now.
